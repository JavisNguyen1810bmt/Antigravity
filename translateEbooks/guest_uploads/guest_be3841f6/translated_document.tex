PART 2: PRODUCT STRATEGY \& TECHNICAL PROBLEM SOLVING
Sarah Jenkins
How do you approach designing AI-driven features where model output is probabilistic rather than deterministic?
How do you manage user expectations and UX around errors?
Alex Morgan
Designing for AI requires a shift from fixed outcomes to confidence intervals. First, we design transparency directly
into the UI—showing confidence scores or explanations for why a suggestion was made. Second, we ensure
seamless human-in-the-loop fallback options. If the confidence threshold falls below 80\%, the UI prompts a quick
manual review rather than automating a decision silently.

PART 2: STRATEGY \& PROBLEM SOLVING (CONTINUED)
Sarah Jenkins
How do you validate product-market fit before committing significant engineering resources to a new AI model?
Alex Morgan
I strictly advocate for 'Wizard of Oz' testing or low-fidelity prototypes. Before building full custom models, we test
demand using third-party APIs or semi-manual processes to observe user engagement. We measure success by key
qualitative indicators like workflow completion speed and qualitative satisfaction, rather than initial vanity metrics.

data. We agreed on an incremental migration strategy, addressing the worst bottlenecks first while continuing to ship
high-impact features every release cycle.
